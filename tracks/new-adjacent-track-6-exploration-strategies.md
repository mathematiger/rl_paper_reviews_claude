## 2026-08-28 (Friday) - Track 6: Exploration strategies (diverse rollout workers)
**Title:** Rethinking Policy Diversity in Ensemble Policy Gradient in Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning
**Authors:** Naoki Shitanda, Motoki Omura, Tatsuya Harada, Takayuki Osa
**Venue/Year:** ICLR 2026 (Poster); arXiv:2603.01741, March 2026 (The University of Tokyo / RIKEN Center for Advanced Intelligence Project)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01741

**Summary:** When RL is scaled to tens of thousands of parallel environments, the bottleneck stops being throughput and becomes the exploration capacity of the single policy all those workers share. Ensemble policy gradient methods answer this by running a population of distinct policies - typically a *leader* whose parameters are the training target and a set of *followers* that fan out into different regions of state space and ship their off-policy samples back to the leader through importance weights. The paper's argument is that the field has treated follower diversity as monotonically good, and that this is wrong: the leader's update consumes follower data through importance-sampling ratios and a PPO-style clip, so as followers drift away from the leader the effective sample size of the aggregated batch collapses, clipping bias grows, and the monotonic-improvement guarantee that justifies the update degrades. The theory makes this a quantified trade-off between exploration breadth and the statistical usability of what exploration returns, rather than a free parameter. Coupled Policy Optimization operationalizes it on top of SAPG with two opposing forces: a KL constraint on each follower's update that tethers it to the leader (bounding the importance ratios), and an adversarial reward term that pushes followers apart so the tether does not collapse the population onto the leader. On large-scale Isaac-Gym-style manipulation, including dexterous manipulation, CPO beats SAPG, PBT/DexPBT and PPO on both sample efficiency and final return; diagnostics show followers settling into a stable ring around the leader with effective sample size held up, and show SAPG suffering severe leader-follower divergence on exactly the tasks where it underperforms.

**Why this, why now:** The user's multi-worker exploration architecture hand-assigns each worker a different behavior - scripted prefixes, epsilon-greedy TD-proxy exploration, temperature sampling - and this paper is the first treatment I have seen that prices the *cost* side of that choice explicitly: the more a worker's behavior policy diverges from the policy being trained, the fewer usable samples its rollouts contribute per unit of wall-clock, via effective sample size and clipping bias. That converts "how aggressive should each worker's exploration setting be" from a hyperparameter swept blindly into a quantity with a diagnostic (ESS per worker) that can be measured mid-training, which is exactly what is needed when rollouts on a power grid simulator are the expensive resource.

**Connection to other papers:** This is the direct counterweight to K-Myriad (logged 2026-08-14), which maximizes collective state entropy across a worker population and treats more population diversity as strictly better; CPO shows that objective has an unpriced cost once the population's data is folded back into one policy by importance weighting, and the two together bracket the design space - K-Myriad for pretraining specialized workers whose data is never merged, CPO for the online case where it is. It also inherits its machinery from SAPG (Singla et al., 2024), which introduced the leader-follower ensemble for massively parallel RL, and sits opposite the ensemble-exploration line of Bootstrapped DQN (logged 2026-08-25): both make workers genuinely diverse rather than independently noisy, but bootstrapping induces diversity through data resampling with no constraint pulling heads together, whereas CPO makes the pull an explicit KL term because its aggregation step, unlike a shared torso, is statistically fragile.

## 2026-08-14 (Friday) - Track 6: Exploration strategies (evolutionary baselines vs RL)
**Title:** K-Myriad: Jump-starting Reinforcement Learning with Unsupervised Parallel Agents
**Authors:** Vincenzo De Paola, Mirco Mutti, Riccardo Zamboni, Marcello Restelli
**Venue/Year:** arXiv preprint arXiv:2601.18580, January 2026 (Politecnico di Milano)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18580

**Summary:** Standard parallelization in deep RL (A3C/IMPALA-style multi-worker rollout collection) speeds up training of a single policy by having many workers sample from the same distribution, wasting the diversity available in a large worker pool. This paper asks whether a pool of parallel workers should instead be specialized before task reward is even introduced, as an unsupervised pretraining step. Building on the authors' earlier ICML 2025 work on maximum-state-entropy exploration for parallel agents (which combined per-agent state entropy with an inter-agent diversity term via a centralized policy-gradient objective), K-Myriad scales this to large populations ("myriads") of agents: it optimizes a portfolio of distinct policies that jointly maximize the collective state entropy induced by the whole population, pushing each worker toward a different, non-redundant region of state space rather than duplicating coverage. The resulting portfolio of specialized exploration policies is then used to jump-start downstream RL - each worker's checkpoint becomes a warm-start initialization for task-specific fine-tuning. On high-dimensional continuous-control benchmarks with large-scale parallelization, the population converges to a broad set of qualitatively distinct behaviors, and initializing task learners from this diverse portfolio improves training efficiency and yields more heterogeneous solutions than initializing from identically-distributed workers.

**Why this, why now:** The user's multi-worker exploration architecture (scripted prefixes, epsilon-greedy TD-proxy exploration, temperature-sampling workers) already hand-assigns each worker a different exploration behavior; K-Myriad reframes exactly that design choice as an optimization problem - learning worker specialization to maximize collective state-entropy coverage instead of hand-tuning per-worker exploration parameters - making it a natural reference point for treating worker diversity itself as a trained objective in the MuZero/Grid2Op multi-worker setup.
