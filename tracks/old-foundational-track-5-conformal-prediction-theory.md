# Old & Foundational - Track 5: Conformal prediction theory (distribution-free coverage guarantees)

## 2026-10-13 (Tuesday) - Track 5: Conformal prediction theory (distribution-free coverage guarantees)
**Title:** Classification with Valid and Adaptive Coverage
**Authors:** Yaniv Romano, Matteo Sesia (both Department of Statistics, Stanford University; equal contribution), Emmanuel J. Candès (Departments of Mathematics and of Statistics, Stanford University)
**Venue/Year:** NeurIPS 2020 (Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 33); arXiv:2006.02544, June 2020
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02544

**Summary** (~250 words): This is the primary source for the adaptive-prediction-set (APS) score. Conformal classification before it used a *homogeneous* rule, C(x) = {y : f̂(y|x) >= t}, applying one global threshold to easy states (one label near probability 1) and hard ones (all labels near 1/|Y|) alike, so it undercovers exactly where the problem is hard. The fix starts from an oracle: knowing π_y(x) = P[Y=y|X=x], sort labels descending and define the generalized conditional quantile L(x;π,τ) = min{c : π_(1)+...+π_(c) >= τ}; the top-L labels at τ = 1-α form the smallest deterministic set with conditional coverage, and a generalized inverse S(x,u;π,τ) that drops the last label with probability V = (Σ_{c<=L} π_(c) - τ)/π_(L) gives the smallest *randomized* set with exact conditional coverage. The conformity score inverts that construction: E(x,y,u;π̂) = min{τ : y ∈ S(x,u;π̂,τ)}, essentially the cumulative mass of labels at least as probable as y, randomized at the boundary, computed from any black-box π̂ - a softmax layer will do, calibrated or not. Because E is uniform conditional on X when π̂ = π, scores are comparable across inputs, unlike earlier adaptive scores. Calibration is plug-in: Algorithm 1 splits the data, takes the ceil((1-α)(1+|I₂|))-th largest hold-out score as τ, and applies S there; Theorem 1 gives marginal coverage >= 1-α and, for distinct scores, <= 1-α + 1/(|I₂|+1). Algorithm 2 swaps splitting for CV+/jackknife+ at >= 1-2α (minus an O(K/n) term), and under consistent π̂ both collapse onto the oracle. On a multinomial simulation with an inhomogeneous feature and on Mice Protein Expression and MNIST, every method hits 90% marginal coverage, but homogeneous conformal classification and conformal quantile classification miss worst-slice conditional coverage - even when handed the true probabilities - while SC/CV+/JK+ attain it at comparable set size with SVC or neural-net base models. Under a random forest all methods undercover, which the authors read as that black box estimating probabilities badly.

**Why this, why now:** This is the paper the APS score in a split-conformal candidate-set pipeline actually comes from, and the mechanism worth reading in the original is the randomization term V: the exact-coverage property of the oracle set, and the near-tight upper bound 1-α + 1/(n+1), both depend on breaking the last label probabilistically, which a deterministic implementation quietly trades for conservatism and larger sets. Its diagnostic framing also matters directly - worst-slice coverage holding under a neural-net head but failing under a random forest is presented as evidence about the *quality of the probability estimates*, which makes per-slice conditional coverage a usable check on a learned probability head rather than just a property of the wrapper around it.

**Connection to other papers:** It is the constructive answer to the impossibility result of Barber, Candès, Ramdas and Tibshirani (Track 5, 2026-09-15), which this paper cites in its second paragraph to justify not chasing exact conditional coverage: since validity cannot supply per-instance guarantees, adaptivity has to be engineered into the nonconformity score, and the generalized inverse quantile is precisely that engineering. It inherits the exchangeability-only p-value machinery of Gammerman, Vovk and Vapnik (Track 5, 2026-08-18), transplants the CV+/jackknife+ calibration of Barber et al. to unordered labels for the first time, and is the categorical counterpart of conformalized quantile regression - the same move from a global threshold to an input-adaptive one, with sorted class probabilities in place of estimated quantiles.

## 2026-09-15 (Tuesday) - Track 5: Conformal prediction theory (distribution-free coverage guarantees)
**Title:** The limits of distribution-free conditional predictive inference
**Authors:** Rina Foygel Barber, Emmanuel J. Candès, Aaditya Ramdas, Ryan J. Tibshirani
**Venue/Year:** Information and Inference: A Journal of the IMA, 10(2):455-482, 2021 (arXiv preprint 2019)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04684 (journal version: https://doi.org/10.1093/imaiai/iaaa017)

**Summary:** Conformal prediction buys finite-sample validity under exchangeability alone, but only *marginally*: coverage holds on average over test points, not for the particular input at hand. This paper asks how much of that gap can be closed without distributional assumptions, and answers mostly negatively. Its central theorem: if a procedure guarantees (1-alpha) coverage conditional on X = x, uniformly over all distributions, then for any distribution with a non-atomic feature marginal the interval it returns has infinite expected length at almost every x. Exact conditional coverage is therefore not merely hard but vacuous - the only distribution-free procedures achieving it are the trivial ones. Earlier special-case versions of this (Vovk; Lei and Wasserman) are generalized here to arbitrary procedures, including randomized ones. The paper then maps the space of relaxations. Requiring coverage conditional on membership in a *collection of subsets* is achievable precisely when the collection is coarse enough - finitely many groups of non-vanishing probability - which is what Mondrian/group-conditional conformal already does. Requiring approximate conditional coverage in shrinking neighborhoods of x, or uniformly over distributions in a neighborhood of P, collapses back onto the impossibility: length again degenerates toward the trivial construction. Positive results survive only once assumptions return (smoothness of the conditional law, or a restricted distribution class), where split-conformal intervals with well-chosen scores are shown to be near-optimal in length.

**Why this, why now:** It draws the exact line under the split-conformal / APS-RAPS pipeline: the coverage certificate on a calibration set is a population-level statement, so any claim that a candidate set covers the true label *in this particular state* is buying something the theory provably cannot sell distribution-free - and the paper names the only two legal ways to recover it, coarse group-conditional calibration or an assumption-backed score whose adaptivity is doing the real work.

**Connection to other papers:** Direct sequel to Gammerman-Vovk-Vapnik's transduction paper (Track 5, 2026-08-18), which established that exchangeability alone yields finite-sample-valid p-values - this paper shows that guarantee is not upgradeable to a per-instance one, so conditional adaptivity must come from the nonconformity score's design rather than from the validity theorem. It also frames the calibration papers of the Track 3/4 lines as complementary: those chase per-instance reliability by accepting model assumptions, precisely the currency this impossibility result says must be spent.
