## 2026-10-14 (Wednesday) - Track 2: Conformal prediction for sequential decision-making
**Title:** Reinforcement Learning with Conformal Action Sets: An Application to Sequential Recommendation
**Authors:** Wenwen Si, Honghao Wei
**Venue/Year:** arXiv:2610.08743 (v1, 6 October 2026); cs.LG / cs.AI preprint, no venue listed
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08743

**Summary** (~250 words): Sequential recommenders commit to a fixed slate size M even though the number of actions worth showing varies state to state. This paper makes that size a calibrated quantity. The target is the oracle near-optimal set A*_eps(s) = {a : Q*(s,a) >= V*(s) - eps}, unobservable, so the method scores actions by the critic's value *gap* g_theta(s,a) = max_b Qhat(s,b) - Qhat(s,a) - shift-invariant in the critic, so an offset in Q does not move the set - and retains the raw set C_{theta,tau}(s) = {a : g_theta(s,a) <= tau}, capped at K (set to the baseline's M) and floored at one action by a lowest-score fallback. The threshold is the only calibrated object: tau_{t+1} = tau_t + eta_t(e_t - alpha), driven by one bit per step. Since Q* is unknown, e_t is a *proxy* miss - whether the raw set fails to intersect the near-maximizers of a proxy score Qtilde (here the simulator's response probability), revealed only as a binary. Theorem 3.1 is a pathwise, adversary-proof bound: |(1/T)sum e_t - alpha| <= (W_T + eta_1)/(eta_T T), no i.i.d. margins, no convergence. Theorem 3.2 is an exact identity splitting regret into *filtering* loss Delta_D(s) (best available minus best retained) and *selection* loss zeta_{pi,D}(s) (best retained minus what the policy picks), each under d^pi; Theorem 3.3 chains it with a proxy-fidelity assumption (lambda, delta_prox) into a finite-H bound whose terms are (1-alpha)eps_H + alpha*Delta^max_H, critic and selector sup-norm error, and a calibration-plus-cap-loss term. Architecture: a critic feeding scores, a slate actor or single-item selector sampling from the admissible set, two timescales (threshold fast, critic slow). On KuaiSim with KuaiRand-Pure and ML-1M, an RLCP variant tops catalog diversity in all 19 configurations (1.11x-5.21x the best of A2C/DDPG/TD3/HAC) with no larger retained sets - and Proposition C.13 concedes the honest caveat: pruning changes nothing for an exactly greedy selector, since the critic's argmax always survives.

**Why this, why now:** This is the closest published instance of recall-oriented action-set pruning calibrated *inside* the RL loop rather than as an offline wrapper, and three mechanisms carry over to candidate selection over a large discrete topology-action space on a power grid: the value-gap score, which is shift-invariant and so insensitive to the usual critic bias; the one-bit threshold recursion tau <- tau + eta(e - alpha), which needs only "did the kept set contain a good action", not calibration labels; and the filtering/selection identity, which says exactly which half of the regret a pruner can be blamed for. The proxy-miss construction is the part to read critically - it is the escape hatch for not knowing Q*, and the whole guarantee degrades through delta_prox.

**Connection to other papers:** The threshold recursion is adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs and Candes) transplanted from coverage of a label to retention of a near-optimal action, which is why Theorem 3.1 inherits ACI's pathwise rather than exchangeability-based character - the same escape from exchangeability that *Conformal Prediction Beyond the Horizon* (Track 2, 2026-08-31) and *Conformal Path Reasoning* (Track 2, 2026-09-30) pay for with weighted subsampling and episode-level calibration instead. It is the operational complement to Zhu et al.'s action-conditional sets (Track 2, 2026-08-17): both tighten what a set must guarantee per action, but Zhu et al. refine the conditioning of a prediction set consumed downstream, whereas here the set *is* the action space the policy acts in, and its target - the near-maximizers of a score - is the APS-style "top of the sorted score vector" cut (Track 5, 2026-10-13) applied to Q-values rather than class probabilities.

## 2026-09-30 (Wednesday) - Track 2: Conformal prediction for sequential decision-making
**Title:** Conformal Path Reasoning: Trustworthy Knowledge Graph Question Answering via Path-Level Calibration
**Authors:** Shuhang Lin, Chuhao Zhou, Xiao Lin, Zihan Dong, Kuan Lu, Zhencan Peng, Jie Yin, Dimitris N. Metaxas
**Venue/Year:** arXiv:2605.08077 (v1 8 May 2026, v2 16 June 2026), 13 pages
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08077

**Summary** (~250 words): Multi-hop reasoning over a graph is a sequential decision problem: at each hop the agent picks which relation to expand, and the set it keeps at hop k decides what is reachable at hop k+1. Prior conformal work here calibrates *per hop*, and the paper's diagnosis is that this is invalid rather than merely loose - because the score at hop k is a function of the previous prediction set, S_k = f(x, C_{k-1}), calibration queries are coupled through a Markov dependency chain and permutation invariance fails. Two fixes follow. First, move the calibration unit: query-exchangeable conformal prediction takes an entire reasoning episode as one calibration point, with nonconformity score S(q) = min over correct-answer-terminating paths of the learned path score (+infinity if none is retrieved), and threshold tau_alpha the (1-alpha) empirical quantile. Queries are i.i.d. and retrieval/scoring deterministic, so exchangeability survives and split-conformal theory gives P(set contains at least one correct path) >= 1 - alpha (Thm 4.1). Second, replace the raw semantic-similarity score, which cannot separate correct paths from plausible wrong ones and so inflates set size, with a learned one: RCVNet, a FiLM-conditioned head over query/relation/path embeddings, trained offline on trajectories collected by *PUCT* tree search - visit-count exploration with a semantic prior - under a pairwise Softplus ranking loss on positives against hard negatives that share a prefix and then diverge. Across WebQSP, CWQ, PQ and PQL this gives roughly 45% higher empirical coverage at 52% smaller sets than conformal baselines; on CWQ at alpha=0.5, 50.2% coverage with 8.35 paths where the prior state of the art needs 120.2 and still misses its guarantee.

**Why this, why now:** Two mechanisms transfer directly. One is the exchangeability argument: whenever a calibrated set at one step determines the candidates available at the next, per-step calibration silently breaks the guarantee, and the fix is to make the whole episode the calibration unit with a min-over-correct-candidates score - the same structural trap that appears in any multi-step candidate-filtering scheme on a graph. The other is that the recall-versus-size frontier is governed far more by *what* is scored than by the conformal wrapper: swapping a similarity heuristic for a head trained on search-generated positive/hard-negative pairs bought both higher coverage and a halving of set size at once, which is the only way out of the usual tradeoff, and the hard negatives are prefix-sharing near-misses - the analogue of two topology configurations on a power grid that agree for several switching steps and then diverge.

**Connection to other papers:** It is the graph-structured counterpart to *Conformal Prediction Beyond the Horizon* (Track 2, 2026-08-31): both diagnose a broken exchangeability assumption inside a sequential process and repair it by re-choosing the calibration unit - weighted subsampling of temporally adjacent replay samples there, whole-query aggregation here. Against Zhu et al.'s action-conditional sets (Track 2, 2026-08-17) it moves in the opposite direction on conditioning granularity: Zhu et al. refine a marginal guarantee down to each individual action, whereas this paper deliberately coarsens from per-hop to per-episode to regain validity, and it borrows the PUCT rule from the Track 1 tree-search lineage to generate calibration data rather than to act.

## 2026-09-16 (Wednesday) - Track 2: Conformal prediction for sequential decision-making
**Title:** Conformal Policy Control
**Authors:** Drew Prinster, Clara Fannjiang, Ji Won Park, Kyunghyun Cho, Anqi Liu, Suchi Saria, Samuel Stanton
**Venue/Year:** International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2026); arXiv:2603.02196, March 2026
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02196

**Summary:** An agent that never deviates from a known-safe reference policy cannot improve; one that deviates too far may violate a constraint badly enough to be pulled out of deployment, ending all future learning. This paper turns "how much deviation is too much?" into a calibration question with a finite-sample answer. The setting - which the authors call *policy control*, distinct from policy evaluation or policy learning - takes a trusted reference policy, an optimized but untested policy, and a user-declared risk tolerance, and returns a deployable interpolation between the two. The mechanism is a likelihood-ratio clip: the proposed policy is regulated by capping the ratio between it and the safe policy at a threshold, so an action the optimized policy wants that departs sharply from the reference is pulled back toward the reference. The key inversion is that calibration data collected under the *safe* policy is used to search over the family of clipped candidate policies indexed by that threshold, selecting the most aggressive one whose risk still provably respects the tolerance - rather than fixing a policy and estimating its risk. Theoretically, the authors generalize conformal risk control to bounded but *non-monotonic* loss functions and to feedback-loop distribution shift, where the deployed policy's own data breaks exchangeability. Experiments cover claim-level false-discovery control in medical long-form QA, constrained active learning on tabular regression, and black-box sequence optimization - safe exploration from the first deployment step, without hyperparameter tuning or a correctly specified model class.

**Why this, why now:** The load-bearing mechanism is calibrating *how far an exploring policy may depart from a trusted scripted baseline*, with a distribution-free risk guarantee obtained from data logged under that baseline - exactly the question raised when exploration workers are seeded with scripted prefixes and then allowed to deviate, and the clip-threshold-as-conformal-parameter trick gives a principled dial for that deviation instead of a hand-tuned epsilon. Its handling of feedback-loop shift (calibration data generated by the very policy being regulated) is the same non-exchangeability obstacle that arises when calibrating on replay data from a live grid-control agent.

**Connection to other papers:** It generalizes Angelopoulos et al.'s Conformal Risk Control (which needs a monotone loss) and builds on Prinster et al.'s weighted/adaptive conformal guarantees under feedback-driven distribution shift, carrying both into a control rather than prediction setting. It is orthogonal to the two Track 2 papers logged on 2026-08-31 and 2026-08-17: those calibrate a *prediction* object (return intervals, action-conditional sets) and leave the decision downstream, whereas this one calibrates the policy itself, making the conformal parameter a knob on behaviour rather than on set size.

## 2026-08-31 (Monday) - Track 2: Conformal prediction for sequential decision-making
**Title:** Conformal Prediction Beyond the Horizon: Distribution-Free Inference for Policy Evaluation
**Authors:** Feichen Gan, Youcun Lu, Yingying Zhang, Yukun Liu
**Venue/Year:** NeurIPS 2025 (poster); arXiv:2510.26026, October 2025
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26026

**Summary:** Policy evaluation usually reports a point estimate of the expected return, but a decision maker who cares about downside risk wants an interval around the return that an individual trajectory will actually realize. Conformal prediction supplies exactly that kind of distribution-free interval, yet its exchangeability assumption fails hard in the infinite-horizon RL setting: the target quantity (the discounted return from a state) is never observed in finite data, transitions inside a trajectory are temporally dependent, and off-policy evaluation adds a distribution shift between the behaviour policy that generated the calibration data and the target policy being evaluated. This paper builds a unified conformal framework that handles all three obstacles for on-policy and off-policy infinite-horizon evaluation. The architecture has two modular pieces. First, a *pseudo-return* construction: the unobserved infinite-horizon return is replaced by a truncated rollout of observed rewards plus a bootstrapped tail supplied by a distributional RL model, so the conformity score has a concrete target whose truncation bias is controllable by horizon length. Second, a *time-aware calibration* strategy that draws calibration points from experience replay via weighted subsampling, thinning temporally adjacent samples and reweighting for the policy shift so that approximate exchangeability is restored. The theory gives coverage lower bounds that explicitly absorb both distributional-model misspecification and importance-weight estimation error, quantified in Wasserstein distance rather than assumed away. Experiments on synthetic MDPs and Mountain Car show markedly better empirical coverage than intervals read directly off a distributional RL head.

**Why this, why now:** The load-bearing mechanism here is calibrating a learned return/value head with split-conformal machinery on data that is temporally dependent and off-policy — the same obstacle that arises when calibration data comes from a replay buffer of correlated grid trajectories rather than an i.i.d. holdout, and the paper's weighted-subsampling fix plus its misspecification-aware coverage bound are directly reusable there.

**Connection to other papers:** It sits downstream of Zhang et al.'s *Conformal Off-Policy Prediction* (AISTATS 2023) and the finite-horizon *Conformal Off-Policy Evaluation in MDPs* line, extending them from finite horizons and observed outcomes to the infinite-horizon case via the pseudo-return trick. It is complementary to the action-conditional risk-averse work logged on 2026-08-17: that paper conditions the guarantee on the action to shape a feasible decision set, whereas this one conditions on a state and calibrates the return distribution itself.

## 2026-08-17 (Monday) - Track 2: Conformal prediction for sequential decision-making
**Title:** Conformal Risk-Averse Decision Making with Action Conditional Guarantee
**Authors:** Zihan Zhu, Shayan Kiyani, George Pappas, Hamed Hassani
**Venue/Year:** International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2026); arXiv:2606.05551, June 2026 (University of Pennsylvania)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05551

**Summary:** Conformal prediction wraps a model's output into a prediction set with a distribution-free coverage guarantee, and recent decision-theoretic work (Kiyani et al., ICML 2025) showed such sets can be translated into optimal risk-averse decision policies - but only with a *marginal* safety guarantee, averaged over the whole action space. A policy built this way can badly under-protect on some actions while wastefully over-protecting on others, since the marginal average hides per-action violations. This paper introduces action-conditional conformal prediction: instead of one pooled coverage guarantee, the prediction-set construction is conditioned explicitly on the action the decision maker is about to take, so the distribution-free coverage guarantee holds separately for each action rather than only on average across all of them. The authors show these action-conditional prediction sets act as a tractable proxy for the feasible decision region of an agent optimizing *action-conditional* value-at-risk, letting different actions carry their own appropriately calibrated risk budget instead of sharing one global bound. To fit this from finite calibration data without assuming a parametric loss model, they derive a principled estimator based on minimizing the pinball (quantile) loss per action, extending conditional-validity conformal techniques to this action-conditional setting. The paper positions this as closing a real gap in the Kiyani et al. framework: marginal guarantees can pass in aggregate while silently failing on specific, possibly safety-critical, actions.

**Why this, why now:** This extends the exact mechanism behind the user's recall-maximizing candidate action selection front - conformal-set construction (split-conformal/APS/RAPS) gating which actions are admitted - from a single pooled coverage/recall guarantee over the whole action space to a guarantee conditioned on each candidate action individually, which matters directly for Grid2Op's large, heterogeneous discrete action space where rare topology-change actions could otherwise be silently under-covered by a marginal-only conformal set even as the aggregate recall target is met.
